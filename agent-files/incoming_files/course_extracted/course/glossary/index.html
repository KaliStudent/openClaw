Course Glossary

Every term used in Reading the Digital World, explained in plain language. Click a letter to jump to that section.

ABCD EFGH ILMN OPQR STUV W
A
AI Chat Tool
A program you can have a text conversation with by typing questions. The AI responds in plain language. Examples: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini.
Algorithm
A set of rules a computer follows to decide what to show you. Social media platforms use algorithms to decide which posts appear in your feed.
Android
The mobile operating system made by Google, used on phones by Samsung, Motorola, Google Pixel, and many others.
App
Short for "application" — a program you run on your phone or computer to do a specific task, like checking email or getting directions.
App Store
Apple's official marketplace for downloading apps on iPhone and iPad. All apps are reviewed by Apple before being listed.
Avatar
A photo or image you choose to represent yourself online — your profile picture on social media, for example.
B
Back Button
A left-pointing arrow that takes you to the previous screen or page — like an undo button for navigation.
Battery Saver / Low Power Mode
A phone mode that reduces background activity to extend battery life. Shows as a yellow battery icon on iPhone, or a battery with a leaf/plus on Android.
Bluetooth
A short-range wireless technology that connects devices (headphones, speakers, keyboards) to your phone without cables.
Browser
A program used to visit websites. Common browsers: Safari (iPhone), Chrome (Google), Firefox. The web address bar at the top is where you type website addresses.
Built-in App
An app that comes pre-installed on your phone by the manufacturer. You do not need to download it.
C
Cellular Data
Internet provided through your phone carrier's network (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, etc.) rather than Wi-Fi. Uses your monthly data allowance.
Cloud Storage
Files stored on internet servers (owned by Google, Apple, etc.) rather than only on your device. Accessible from any phone, tablet, or computer by signing in.
Control Center
A panel on iPhone that gives one-tap access to common settings. Open it by swiping down from the top-right corner on newer iPhones.
D
Direct Message (DM)
A private message sent to one person on a social media platform — not visible to anyone else. The envelope or paper-plane icon usually opens your DMs.
Do Not Disturb
A phone setting that silences incoming calls and notifications. Shown as a moon icon (🌙) in the status bar.
Dynamic Island
The pill-shaped cutout at the top of newer iPhones (iPhone 14 Pro and later) that displays alerts and activity — charging status, timers, calls, music — in an animated bubble.
E
Emoji
Small colorful pictures inserted into text messages or posts to convey emotion — 😊 ❤️ 🎉. Access them via the smiley face icon on your phone keyboard.
F
Featured Snippet
A highlighted answer box that appears at the top of Google search results, showing a direct answer to your question without clicking a link.
Feed
The scrolling list of posts, photos, and updates you see when you open a social media app. Determined by the platform's algorithm based on who you follow.
Filter (Search)
A clickable button that narrows search results to a specific type (Images, Videos, News) or time period. Found below the Google search bar after a search.
Focus Mode
An iPhone feature that silences notifications based on your activity (Sleep, Work, Personal). See the moon icon in the status bar when active.
G
Google Account
A free account tied to a Gmail address. Used to access Gmail, Google Maps, Google Drive, YouTube, and all other Google services with a single login.
Google Lens
A Google feature that searches by image. Point your phone camera at any object, text, plant, or product to identify it or find information about it.
Glyph
A single visual symbol or icon — one character in a symbol system. The trash can icon is a glyph; so is the heart emoji.
H
Hamburger Menu (☰)
Three horizontal lines stacked on top of each other. Tapping reveals a hidden navigation menu. Called "hamburger" because the lines look like a burger's layers.
Hotspot
Using your phone as a Wi-Fi router to share your cellular internet connection with a laptop or tablet nearby.
I
Icon
A small picture or symbol on a screen that represents a function, app, or action. Tapping or clicking it performs that action.
iMessage
Apple's internet-based messaging system. Messages between Apple devices are sent as iMessages (blue bubbles) for free, even internationally, when Wi-Fi or cellular data is available.
iOS
The operating system that runs on iPhones and iPads. Made by Apple. Updated once a year with new features.
L
Like
Tapping the thumbs-up or heart icon on a social media post to show appreciation or agreement. Does not notify you of future replies.
Lock Screen
The screen you see before unlocking your phone. Shows the time, date, and notification previews. On iPhones you can access the flashlight and camera from the lock screen.
M
Microphone Icon (🎙)
Appears in search bars and messaging apps to let you speak instead of type. Also appears as a privacy indicator (orange dot on iPhone) when an app is using your microphone.
N
Navigation Bar
The row of buttons (Back, Home, Recent Apps) at the bottom of an Android phone used to move between apps and screens.
Notification
An alert from an app that appears as a pop-up, banner, or number badge — telling you something requires your attention (new message, reminder, news alert).
Notification Center
On iPhone, the panel that collects all past notifications. Open it by swiping down from the top-left corner of the screen.
Notification Shade
On Android, the panel that slides down when you swipe from the top of the screen, showing recent notifications and Quick Settings tiles.
O
Operating System
The core software that runs your phone or computer and manages all its features. iPhone uses iOS; most other phones use Android.
P
Profile Picture
The photo that represents your account on social media, email, or any online service. Usually a circle with your face or chosen image.
Q
Quick Settings
On Android, a panel of tile-shaped shortcut icons for common settings (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, flashlight). Accessed by pulling down the notification shade twice.
R
Regenerate
In AI chat tools, a button that asks the AI to write a completely new response to your same question — useful when the first answer was not quite right.
Repost / Retweet
Sharing someone else's post on X (Twitter) or other platforms so your own followers can see it. Indicated by a two-arrows circular icon (🔁).
S
Share Icon
A box with an upward arrow (iOS) or a chain-link or forward arrow (Android/web). Tapping it lets you share content via text, email, or copy a link.
SMS
Short Message Service — a standard text message sent over the cellular network. Shown as green bubbles in iPhone Messages. May count against your phone plan depending on your carrier.
Status Bar
The thin strip at the very top of any smartphone screen showing battery level, signal strength, Wi-Fi status, time, and system alert icons.
Story (Social Media)
A temporary photo or video post that disappears after 24 hours, available on Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp. Shown as a colored ring around your profile picture.
Subreddit
A specific community within Reddit dedicated to one topic. Named with "r/" prefix (e.g., r/cooking, r/news). Each subreddit has its own rules and moderators.
T
Three Dots (⋮ or ⋯)
A universal "More Options" icon found in almost every app and website. Tapping it reveals a hidden menu of additional actions.
Toggle
A switch you tap to turn a setting on or off — like a light switch. Common in Settings menus. Often shown as a small oval that slides between gray (off) and colored (on).
U
Upvote / Downvote
Reddit's system for rating posts. Upvote (⬆) = valuable or interesting. Downvote (⬇) = not helpful or off-topic. Net votes determine how prominently a post is shown.
V
Voice Search
Speaking a search query out loud instead of typing it. Activated by the microphone icon in Google Search or by saying "Hey Google" / "Hey Siri".
W
Wi-Fi
Wireless internet provided by a router — in your home, a coffee shop, library, or other location. Uses no cellular data and is usually faster than cellular.
