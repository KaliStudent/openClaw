Module 4

Google Search Icons

⏱ 15 minutes 📘 Beginner Lesson 4 of 20

What You Will Learn

  • Recognize every icon on the Google Search results page
  • Understand what the microphone and camera icons do
  • Use the "Tools" and filter icons to sharpen your searches
  • Know the difference between organic results and paid ads

The Google Search Bar

When you open Google at google.com, the first thing you see is a white search bar — a long rectangle in the middle of the page. Inside or beside that bar sit two small icons that many people never notice. Once you know what they do, searching becomes much faster and easier.

🎙️
Microphone
Search by speaking. Tap this, say your question out loud, and Google types it for you. Perfect if typing is difficult.
📷
Camera / Google Lens
Search by picture. Point your camera at any object, plant, animal, product, or text, and Google identifies it.
🔍
Magnifying Glass
The "Go" button. After typing your search, click or tap this to start the search. You can also just press Enter on a keyboard.
Tip: Voice search works extremely well for longer questions. Instead of typing "best restaurants near me open now Sunday," just tap the microphone and say it naturally.

Google Lens — Searching With Your Camera

Google Lens is one of the most powerful tools most people have never tried. When you tap the camera icon inside the search bar, your phone camera opens. Here is what you can do:

  • Point at a plant — Google identifies the species and tells you how to care for it.
  • Point at a product — Google finds where to buy it and compares prices.
  • Point at a restaurant menu in another language — Google translates it instantly.
  • Point at a math problem — Google solves it step by step.

Icons on the Search Results Page

After you search, a page of results appears. This page has several icons and labels. Let's walk through them from top to bottom.

Icon / LabelWhat It MeansShould You Click It?
"Sponsored" or "Ad" badge A company paid Google to appear at the top. It is not the most relevant result — it's advertising. Optional — ads can be useful but scroll past them first to see genuine results.
Three-dot menu (⋮) beside a result Opens options to hide similar results, report a misleading page, or learn about the source. Yes, if a result looks suspicious and you want to report it.
Globe 🌐 Shown in some results to indicate a website (as opposed to a local place on a map). Informational — no action needed.
Map Pin 📍 Result is a physical location (restaurant, store, clinic). Tapping opens Google Maps. Yes, if you want directions or the phone number.
Star ratings ★★★★☆ Average customer review score for a business. Out of 5 stars. Below the stars you will see the number of reviews in parentheses. Informational — helps you decide if a business is worth visiting.
Share icon ↗ Copy the link to a search result so you can send it to someone via text or email. Yes, when you want to share a page with family or friends.
Cached / More options A saved copy of the page, useful if the original site is temporarily down. Rarely — only if the site won't load.

The Filter Bar — Narrowing Your Results

Just below the search bar, after you search, you will see a row of clickable words or tabs. These are filters. They are not icons exactly, but they look like small buttons. Common ones include:

🌐
All
Default view. Shows web pages, images, videos, and news all mixed together.
🖼
Images
Shows only pictures related to your search. Great for seeing what something looks like.
▶️
Videos
Shows only video results, mostly from YouTube. Great for how-to tutorials.
📰
News
Shows recent news articles about your search topic. Sorted by newest first.
🗺
Maps
Shows a map view of nearby places matching your search. Best for "near me" searches.
🛒
Shopping
Shows products for sale with prices. Good for comparing costs before buying something.

You will also see a "Tools" button on desktop (or a sliders icon on mobile). Tapping Tools lets you filter by time — for example, showing only results from the past week, or the past year. This is very useful when you want current news and not old articles.

The "Featured Snippet" Box

Sometimes Google places a blue or white box at the very top of results with a direct answer to your question. This is called a featured snippet. It has no special icon, but you will recognize it by its position (first) and its boxed appearance. You can read the answer right there without clicking anything. If you want more detail, click the link below the box to visit the full page.

Safety Note: Google shows you results, but it does not guarantee every website is safe. Before clicking any result, look at the web address (the green or gray text below the blue link title). Addresses ending in .gov (government), .edu (university), or from well-known names like mayo clinic, webmd, or nytimes are generally reliable.
