Module 6

Android Navigation & System Icons

⏱ 15 minutes 📘 Beginner Lesson 10 of 20

What You Will Learn

  • Navigate confidently using the Back, Home, and Recent Apps buttons
  • Understand both gesture navigation and button navigation on Android
  • Use Quick Settings tiles to change phone settings instantly
  • Understand the Google Assistant button and how to trigger it

The Navigation Bar — The Row at the Bottom

On many Android phones, a row of icons or invisible zones sits at the very bottom of the screen. This is the navigation bar. It contains your three most essential phone controls. Depending on your phone model and settings, these may appear as visible buttons or as invisible zones you swipe on.

◀
Back Button
A left-pointing triangle or arrow. Takes you one step backward — like an undo button. Closes menus, returns to previous screens, or exits apps. On gesture phones: swipe inward from either edge.
⬤
Home Button
A circle or house icon in the center. Always takes you back to your home screen, no matter where you are. On gesture phones: swipe up from the bottom center and release.
▭
Recent Apps / Overview
A square or stack of rectangles. Shows all currently open apps as cards you can swipe through. Swipe a card up to close that app. On gesture phones: swipe up from the bottom and hold briefly.
Two Navigation Styles: Older Android phones show three visible buttons. Newer Android phones (and many Samsung phones in the default setting) use gesture navigation — no visible buttons, just swipe movements. Both do the same job.

Quick Settings Tiles

When you pull down the notification shade and then swipe down a second time, you expand the Quick Settings panel. Each tile controls one feature. Tap once to toggle it on or off. Press and hold to open its full settings page.

Tile IconNameWhat It Does
🛜 Wi-Fi Toggle Wi-Fi on/off. Tap-and-hold to choose a different network.
📶 Mobile Data Turn cellular internet on/off. Turn off when on Wi-Fi to save your data allowance.
🔵 Bluetooth Toggle Bluetooth. Tap-and-hold to pair a new device.
✈ Airplane Mode Turn off all wireless radios instantly.
🔦 Flashlight Turn your camera LED into a flashlight. Very useful, instantly accessible.
🌙 Do Not Disturb Silence all calls and notifications. Tap-and-hold to schedule it or set exceptions.
🔄 Auto-Rotate Allow (or lock) the screen from rotating when you turn the phone sideways.
☀ Brightness A slider or tap to enable Auto-Brightness (adjusts to room lighting automatically).
🔋 Battery Saver Enable low-power mode to extend battery life by reducing background activity.
🔗 Hotspot Share your phone's internet with a nearby laptop or tablet via Wi-Fi. Uses cellular data.

Google Assistant

Most Android phones include Google Assistant, a voice-controlled AI helper. You can trigger it several ways:

  • Say "Hey Google" out loud if voice activation is enabled
  • Long-press the Home button (on older phones)
  • Swipe inward from the bottom corner (on some phones)
  • Press a dedicated Google Assistant button on the side of some Samsung or other brand phones

When Assistant activates, you will see a colored wave of dots at the bottom of the screen. Ask it anything: "What is the weather tomorrow?", "Call Mom", "Set a timer for 10 minutes", or "Remind me to take my medicine at 8am."

Tip: Google Assistant understands natural language. You do not need to use special commands. Speak to it the same way you would talk to another person.

The Google Pixel — Pure Android

If you have a Google Pixel phone, your Android is the "purest" version — closest to how Google originally designed it, without the customizations that Samsung or Motorola add. The icons look slightly more minimal and clean. But all the same rules in this course apply.

One unique icon on Pixel phones: the Now Playing icon — a small music note appears in the status bar when your phone quietly identifies a song playing nearby, even without you asking. Tapping it shows the song name.
