# Email & Meeting Summary — Worked Examples

> Concrete walkthroughs showing inbox triage, meeting summary, response drafting, and digest delivery.

## Example 1: Morning Inbox Triage

**User:** "Catch me up on email — what came in overnight?"

**Agent pulls inbox (12 unread messages). Delivers:**

```markdown
# Inbox Summary — Wednesday, July 16

**Unread:** 12 | **Needs You:** 3 | **FYI:** 5 | **Archive:** 4

---

## 🔴 Needs You (3)

### Jess Rivera — "Revised scope question"
**Ask:** Wants to know if adding SEO to their website package changes the timeline.
  She has a board meeting Friday and needs to confirm deliverables.
**Deadline:** Needs answer by Thursday EOD (tomorrow)
**Context:** Existing client, website redesign project. You discussed adding SEO last call.
**Suggested response:** "Hi Jess — adding SEO would extend timeline by ~2 weeks.
  I can send a revised SOW by tomorrow with the updated dates. Would that work for
  your board prep?"

### Marcus Reeves — "Henderson situation update"
**Ask:** Henderson client threatened to leave. Marcus needs your guidance on whether
  to offer a discount or hold firm.
**Deadline:** Meeting with Henderson is tomorrow at 10 AM
**Context:** Henderson has been unhappy since the delayed deliverable. Marcus is your
  account manager handling this.
**Suggested response:** Don't email — call Marcus. This needs a 5-minute verbal discussion.

### Alex Tan (Accountant) — "Missing receipts for June"
**Ask:** Needs 3 missing receipts to close June books. Listed the transactions.
**Deadline:** "Need by Friday to file on time"
**Context:** Monthly bookkeeping. Alex sends this every month.

## 👀 Watch (0)

Nothing developing. Clean slate.

## 📋 Action Items

| 1 | Reply to Jess re: SEO timeline | You | Thu EOD | Email |
| 2 | Call Marcus re: Henderson discount decision | You | Today | Email |
```

## Example 2: Meeting Transcript Summary

**User:** "Here are notes from my client call today." [Pastes transcript]

**Transcript (abridged):**
> You: Thanks for jumping on, Lisa. Wanted to check in on the project and discuss next steps.
> Lisa: Sure! Overall happy with progress. The homepage looks great. I do have concerns about mobile.
> You: What specifically?
> Lisa: The navigation feels clunky on phone. Also the contact form is hard to tap on smaller screens.
> You: Noted. We can address both in the next sprint. Should be done by end of next week.
> Lisa: Perfect. Oh — also, my CEO wants to see a demo before we go live. Can we schedule that?
> You: Absolutely. I'll send some times for a demo. When does he need to see it by?

> Lisa: Nope! Oh wait — can you also send me the analytics setup plan? I want to share it with our marketing person.

## Example 3: Long Email Thread Summarization

**User:** "Summarize this thread — it's gotten long and I've lost track." [Pastes 18-message email chain]

**Agent delivers:**

```markdown
# Thread Summary: "Q4 Marketing Budget Approval"

**Participants:** You, Dana (CFO), Mike (Marketing Director), Rachel (VP Sales)
**Duration:** 8 days (July 8-16), 18 messages
**Current status:** Waiting on you.

---

## The Ask Right Now
Dana is waiting for your final sign-off on the revised budget ($85K, down from Mike's
original $120K request). She needs it today to include in the board package.

## How We Got Here
1. **July 8:** Mike proposed $120K Q4 marketing budget (events + paid ads + new hire)
2. **July 10:** Dana pushed back — said max budget is $90K based on cash flow projections
3. **July 11-13:** Back-and-forth between Mike and Dana. Mike argued events alone need $50K.
   Dana suggested cutting the new hire and reallocating to contractors.
4. **July 14:** Rachel jumped in supporting higher budget — says sales pipeline needs marketing fuel
5. **July 15:** You asked Mike to prioritize. He came back with $85K version (no new hire,
   reduced events to $35K, kept paid ads at $30K, $20K for content/contractors)
6. **July 16 (today):** Dana said $85K works if you approve. Waiting on your go/no-go.

## Key Decisions Made
- New marketing hire deferred to Q1 (Mike and Dana agreed)
- Events budget capped at $35K (Mike's revised proposal)
- Contractor budget allocated for content ($20K)

## What's Still Open
- **Your approval needed** — Reply yes/no to Dana's email

**Suggested response to Dana:**

> "Approved — $85K Q4 marketing budget as Mike proposed. Go ahead and include
> in the board package. Mike — please send the specific event plan by next week
> so we can review allocations."

_Ready to send, or want to adjust?_
```

## Example 5: Response Drafting with Insufficient Context

**User:** "Draft a reply to Mike's email about the conference sponsorship."

**Agent checks email:** Mike's email asks "Are we doing the TechConnect conference in October?
The early-bird sponsorship deadline is July 25. Gold sponsor is $15K, Silver is $8K.
Should I register us?"

**Agent delivers:**

```markdown
**Draft reply to Mike re: TechConnect Conference:**

I have two concerns before drafting:

1. **Budget:** The Q4 marketing budget was just approved at $85K with $35K for events.
   $15K for one sponsorship is 43% of the events budget. Do you want Gold or Silver?
2. **Strategy fit:** I don't have context on whether TechConnect is a priority event
   for you. Has the team attended before? Is your target audience there?

**If you can tell me:**
- Gold ($15K) or Silver ($8K)?
- Is this a priority event?

**Then I'd draft something like:**

> _"Mike — yes, let's go with [Gold/Silver]. Go ahead and register before the early-bird
> deadline. Can you put together a brief plan for what we want to get out of it (leads
> target, speaking opportunities, etc.)? That'll help us decide how to staff it."_

**Or if you're not sure:**

> _"Mike — thanks for flagging the deadline. Can you put together a quick 1-pager on
> what we'd get out of TechConnect? Who attends, what's our goal, what did we get last
> time (if we've been before)? Want to make sure it's the right use of the events budget
> before committing. Need it by [date] to beat the deadline."_

Which direction feels right?
```

## Key Patterns Across Examples

1. **Always lead with "the ask right now"** — In any summary, the first thing the principal
   reads should be what they need to do next. Background comes second.

2. **Action items are sacred** — Never bury them in prose. Always extract into a clear table
   with owner + deadline. If a deadline wasn't stated, note that explicitly.

3. **Drafted responses explain the approach** — Don't just write the draft. Explain why you
   wrote it that way so the principal can adjust the strategy, not just the words.

4. **Know when NOT to draft** — If you're missing critical context, ask instead of guessing.
   A wrong draft wastes more time than no draft.

5. **Scheduled outputs are shorter and more scannable** — Morning digests and EOD wraps
   should take <60 seconds to read. Use emoji, bullets, and bold liberally.
