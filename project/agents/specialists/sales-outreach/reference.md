# Sales Outreach — Reference

Detailed rules, cadence templates, objection handling, and operational protocols for the Sales Outreach agent.

---

## Email Writing Rules

### Subject Line Principles

**What works:**
- Short (4-7 words max)
- Lowercase feels personal (not auto-generated)
- Specific reference beats generic value prop
- Questions that are easy to answer
- The prospect's name or company name

**What doesn't work:**
- ALL CAPS or excessive punctuation (!!!)
- "Quick question" (overused, now signals spam)
- Clickbait that doesn't match the body
- Long subject lines that get cut off on mobile
- "Following up" as a subject (lazy)

**Subject line formulas:**
| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| [Mutual connection] mentioned you | Alex mentioned you might need... |
| question about [their thing] | question about your new location |
| [company] + [your company] | Riverside Dental + [Your Service] |
| idea for [specific challenge] | idea for managing your summer rush |
| [their recent event] | congrats on the expansion |
| noticed [observation] | noticed you're hiring 3 new techs |

### Email Body Structure

**The 3-sentence cold email (highest reply rates):**
1. **Personalization** — Why you're emailing THEM specifically
2. **Value** — What you do and why it matters to them
3. **Ask** — Clear, low-commitment next step

**Maximum length:** 150 words for cold outreach. Every additional word reduces reply rate.

**Formatting rules:**
- No images in first cold email (triggers spam filters)
- No more than 1 link
- No attachments
- Plain text looks more personal than HTML
- Mobile-first: keep lines under 60 characters
- One CTA. Only one. Don't give them choices.

### Words to Avoid in Cold Email

These trigger spam filters or pattern-match as "sales email":
- "Just following up"
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out because..."
- "We're the leading..."
- "Innovative/cutting-edge/game-changing"
- "Synergize/leverage/optimize"
- "I'd love to pick your brain"
- "Free" in subject line
- "Limited time offer"
- "No obligation"

### Words That Work

- Their name (obviously)
- Specific details about their business
- "Quick question" → replaced with an ACTUAL quick question
- Numbers and specifics ("3 other shops on Main St use us")
- Casual language ("hey", "btw", "heads up")
- Questions they can answer yes/no

## Cadence Templates by Industry

### Template: Home Services → Homeowners

**Use for:** Plumbers, HVAC, roofers, landscapers reaching property managers or homeowners
**Sequence length:** 4 touches over 10 days

| Day | Channel | Message Focus |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| 0 | Email | Local reference + seasonal pain point |
| 3 | Text | Ultra-short follow-up with phone number |
| 6 | Email | Before/after result from neighbor or nearby project |
| 10 | Email | Breakup with soft offer |

**Key notes:**
- Homeowners respond to TEXT more than email
- Local proof (neighbors, same neighborhood) is the #1 converter
- Seasonal timing matters enormously (AC before summer, heating before winter)

### Template: Professional Services → Business Owners

**Use for:** Accountants, lawyers, consultants, marketers reaching small biz owners
**Sequence length:** 6 touches over 18 days

| Day | Channel | Message Focus |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| 0 | Email | Observation about their business + question |
| 3 | LinkedIn | Connection request with note |
| 5 | Email | Mini case study from similar business |
| 9 | Email | Specific resource or tip (give value) |
| 13 | Phone | Brief call — 30 seconds or voicemail |
| 18 | Email | Breakup |

**Key notes:**
- Business owners are email-heavy — subject lines matter more
- LinkedIn connection BEFORE email sequence can warm the intro
- Giving genuine value (a tip, resource, observation) builds reciprocity

### Template: B2B Service → Decision Maker

**Use for:** Software vendors, agencies, suppliers reaching VP/Director-level contacts
**Sequence length:** 7 touches over 21 days

| Day | Channel | Message Focus |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| 0 | Email | Personalized observation + question |
| 2 | LinkedIn | View profile + connection request |
| 4 | Email | Data point or trend relevant to their role |
| 7 | Email | Customer story from their industry |
| 11 | Phone | Call attempt + voicemail |
| 14 | Email | Different angle — new value prop |
| 21 | Email | Breakup |

### Template: Local Referral Ask

**Use for:** Asking existing customers/partners for referrals
**Sequence length:** 2 touches over 5 days

| Day | Channel | Message Focus |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| 0 | Email/Text | Thank them + specific ask ("know anyone who...") |
| 5 | Email/Text | Gentle reminder + make it easy (forward template) |

## Follow-Up Strategy

### The "New Value" Rule

Every follow-up must add something new. Never send "just checking in" or "wanted to bump this." Each touch needs a reason to exist:

| Touch # | New Value to Add |
|---------|------------------|
| 2 | Additional context, a clarifying question |
| 3 | Case study, result, or proof point |
| 4 | Resource, article, or insight relevant to them |
| 5 | Different angle on the same problem |
| 6 | Social proof (another customer in their space) |
| 7 | Breakup — close the loop gracefully |

### Follow-Up After Meetings

**Same day (within 2 hours):**
- Thank them for their time
- Recap what was discussed (3-5 bullet points)
- State clear next steps with dates
- Attach any promised materials

**If they go silent after meeting:**
- Day 3: Short check-in, reference a specific point from the conversation
- Day 7: Provide new value (relevant article, case study, answer to a question from the meeting)
- Day 14: Direct question about timeline/status
- Day 21: Breakup — acknowledge they're busy, leave door open

### The Breakup Email

The breakup email often gets the highest reply rate because it:
1. Removes pressure (you're stopping the sequence)
2. Triggers loss aversion (they might lose the opportunity)
3. Shows professionalism (you respect their time)

**Structure:**
```
Subject: should I close your file?

[Name] —

I've reached out a few times and haven't heard back, so I'm guessing the timing isn't right.

Totally fine. I'll close out our conversation for now.

If [the need] comes up later, you know where to find me.

[Your name]
```

## Lead List Prioritization

### Priority Scoring Framework

Score each prospect 1-5 on these dimensions:

| Dimension | What to Assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Fit** | How well they match the ideal customer profile |
| **Timing** | Signals of current or imminent need |
| **Access** | Ability to reach the decision-maker |
| **Size** | Potential deal value |
| **Competition** | Whether they're already served by a competitor |

**Priority tiers:**
- **Hot (Score 18-25):** Contact within 24 hours. Strong fit + timing signals.
- **Warm (Score 12-17):** Include in weekly outreach batch. Good fit, timing unclear.
- **Cold (Score 5-11):** Nurture list. Monitor for timing signals.

### Timing Signals (What to Watch For)

| Signal | What It Means | Source |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| New hire in relevant role | Budget allocated, initiative starting | Job postings, LinkedIn |
| Expansion/new location | Growing, spending money | News, social media |
| Leadership change | New priorities, open to new vendors | LinkedIn, press |
| Competitor leaving market | Gap to fill | Industry news |
| Seasonal trigger | Predictable need arising | Calendar, weather |
| Technology change | Integration opportunity | Job posts, tech stack data |
| Negative review of competitor | Dissatisfaction = opportunity | Google reviews, G2 |
| Funding round | Money to spend | Crunchbase, news |

## Objection Handling in Outreach

### Common Objections + Responses

**"We already have someone for that"**
- Acknowledge: "Makes sense — most [industry] businesses do."
- Pivot: "Quick question: are they handling [specific pain point]? I'm asking because that's the #1 thing businesses like yours switch for."
- Or: "No worries. If things change, I'm here. Mind if I check back in [90 days]?"

**"Not interested"**
- Accept: "Totally understand. Thanks for letting me know."
- Optional soft close: "Any chance you'd tell me what made you say that? Helps me not waste other people's time too."
- Remove from sequence immediately.

**"Send me more info"**
- This is usually a brush-off but treat it as an opening
- Send ONE concise piece (not a brochure dump)
- Follow up in 2-3 days: "Did that answer your question about [specific thing]?"

**"We don't have the budget"**
- Acknowledge: "I hear that a lot this time of year."
- Reframe: "What if I showed you how [specific savings/revenue]? Might change the math."
- Or: "When does your budget cycle reset? I'll reach back out then."

**"I need to think about it"**
- Pin to specifics: "Totally fair. What specifically would help you decide? I might have something that answers it."
- Set a timeline: "Makes sense. Want me to check back [Wednesday/next week]?"

**Silence (no reply at all)**
- This is the most common outcome. Don't take it personally.
- Follow the cadence. After full sequence, stop.
- Re-engage in 60-90 days with a new angle/trigger.

## Text/SMS Outreach Rules

### When to Use Text
- After an email has been sent (follow-up medium)
- For time-sensitive offers or reminders
- When the prospect is a small business owner (they live on their phone)
- For appointment confirmations
- NEVER as the first cold touch (too invasive)

### Text Formatting
- Max 160 characters if possible (avoids splitting)
- Always identify yourself: "Hey [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]"
- One question or one CTA per text
- Casual tone — texts should feel like texts, not emails

### Text Templates

**Follow-up after email:**
> Hey [Name], it's [you] from [business]. Sent you an email Tuesday about [topic] — worth a look? Happy to chat whenever works.

**After a meeting:**
> Great meeting today [Name]. I'll send over the [thing] we discussed by EOD. Anything else on your mind?

**Appointment reminder:**
> Hey [Name] — just confirming our call tomorrow at [time]. Still work for you?

## Voice/Phone Scripts

### Cold Call Opening (30 seconds max)

```
"Hey [Name], this is [Your name] from [Business]. 

I know I'm catching you out of the blue — I'll be quick. 

I work with [industry] businesses in [area] to help them [primary value]. 
Saw you're [personalization — expanding, hiring, etc.].

Worth a 5-minute conversation to see if we could help? 
If not, totally fine — I'll let you go."
```

### Voicemail Script (under 20 seconds)

```
"Hey [Name], it's [Your name] with [Business]. 

Quick message — I help [industry] businesses with [specific thing] 
and had an idea for [their company]. 

I'll shoot you an email with details. My number is [number] if you want to call back. 
Hope you're having a good [day of week]."
```

### Key Phone Rules
- NEVER read a script word-for-word (it sounds terrible)
- Use the script as talking points
- Smile when talking (people hear it)
- If voicemail, keep under 20 seconds
- Follow up the call with a text or email referencing the call

## Confirmation Protocol

### Before Any Outbound Action

This protocol applies to ALL sends:

1. **Present** — Show the exact message, recipient, and channel
2. **Context** — Explain why this person, why this angle, why now
3. **Confirm** — Get explicit "send it" or "approved"
4. **Send** — Only after approval
5. **Log** — Record what was sent, when, and to whom

### Batch Approval

For sequences with multiple touches:
1. Present the full sequence (all messages, timing, channels)
2. Ask: "Approve the full sequence, or want to edit individual messages?"
3. Once approved, execute on schedule
4. Report back: "Sent email 1 to [Name] at [time]. Next touch scheduled for [date]."

### Kill Switch

If at ANY point the prospect:
- Replies "unsubscribe" or "stop"
- Says "not interested" or "remove me"
- Seems angry or threatened

**Immediately:**
1. Stop all scheduled outreach
2. Remove from all sequences
3. Notify the business owner
4. Send a polite acknowledgment to the prospect if appropriate

## Pipeline Management

### The Needs You / Handled / Watch Framework

Review pipeline weekly (or on request) using this structure:

**🔴 Needs You** — Things only the business owner can act on:
- Prospect replied and needs a human response
- Meeting to confirm or reschedule
- Contract/proposal to send
- Decision about whether to continue pursuing

**🟢 Handled** — Things in motion:
- Sequence running on schedule
- Follow-up sent, waiting for reply
- Meeting confirmed for future date
- Proposal out, awaiting response

**🟡 Watch** — Things to monitor:
- Prospect went silent mid-sequence
- Timing signal detected for nurture contact
- Competitor mentioned by prospect
- Re-engagement candidates (60+ days since last sequence)

### Pipeline Hygiene Rules

- Move prospects to "Closed - No Reply" after full sequence completes with no response
- Move prospects to "Closed - Lost" after explicit rejection
- Never keep dead prospects in active pipeline (it obscures real opportunities)
- Review "Watch" list every 2 weeks for re-engagement opportunities
- "Won" means a meeting was booked or deal closed — be specific about what "won" means for this business

## Industry-Specific Notes

### Trades (Plumbing, HVAC, Electrical)
- Reaching: Property managers, real estate agents, commercial building owners
- Best channel: Phone > Text > Email (these people are rarely at a desk)
- Key hook: Speed of response, reliability, licensing/insurance
- Timing: Seasonal (AC in spring, heating in fall)

### Professional Services (Accounting, Legal, Marketing)
- Reaching: Business owners, CFOs, office managers
- Best channel: Email > LinkedIn > Phone
- Key hook: Time savings, compliance/risk, ROI
- Timing: Budget cycles, tax season, year-end planning

### Medical/Dental
- Reaching: Practice managers, not doctors (doctors don't read sales emails)
- Best channel: Email > Phone to front desk
- Key hook: Patient satisfaction, efficiency, revenue per chair
- Timing: After insurance resets (January), slow months

### Restaurants/Food Service
- Reaching: Owners directly (small) or GMs (larger)
- Best channel: Text > Walk-in > Email
- Key hook: Foot traffic, cost savings, time savings
- Timing: Before busy seasons, during slow months (they have time to think)

### Real Estate
- Reaching: Agents, brokers, property managers
- Best channel: Text > Phone > Email (agents live on phone)
- Key hook: Listings, leads, transaction speed
- Timing: Spring market ramp-up, post-closing follow-up
