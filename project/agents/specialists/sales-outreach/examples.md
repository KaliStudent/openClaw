# Sales Outreach — Worked Examples

## Example 1: HVAC Company — Cold Outreach to Property Managers

### Request
> "I run a commercial HVAC company in Dallas. I want to reach out to property management companies that manage apartment complexes. We do maintenance contracts and emergency repair. Help me build a sequence."

### Research Phase

I research the prospect (fictional example):
- **Company:** Greenfield Property Management
- **Manages:** 12 apartment complexes in North Dallas (2,800+ units)
- **Contact:** Maria Delgado, Director of Facilities
- **Personalization hook:** They just took over management of a new 240-unit complex (found in press release)
- **Priority score:** Hot (22/25) — growth signal + perfect fit + reachable contact

### Output

---

## Outreach Sequence: Greenfield Property Management

**Target:** Maria Delgado, Director of Facilities
**Goal:** Book a 15-minute intro call about maintenance contract
**Duration:** 14 days
**Touches:** 5 (Email, Text, Email, Phone, Email)

---

### Touch 1: Email (Day 1)

**Subject:** the new 240-unit complex
**Send time:** Tuesday 8:30 AM

---

Maria —

Saw that Greenfield just took over Parkside Terrace — congrats. 240 units is a lot of HVAC to inherit, especially heading into summer.

I run Apex HVAC here in Dallas. We maintain the systems for 6 property management companies in North Texas (about 4,000 units total). The short version: one contract, guaranteed response times, and you never get a 2 AM surprise that nobody's available for.

Would 15 minutes this week make sense to see if we're a fit? I can share what we do for properties similar to Parkside.

— Jake Torres
Apex HVAC | (214) 555-0172

---

**Why this angle:** The new property acquisition is a timing signal — she's actively thinking about vendors for that building. Leading with it shows I'm paying attention, not blasting a list.

---

### Touch 2: Text (Day 3)

> Hey Maria, it's Jake from Apex HVAC. Shot you an email Tuesday about HVAC maintenance for Greenfield's properties — especially with Parkside coming online. Worth a quick call? (214) 555-0172

---

### Touch 3: Email (Day 6)

**Subject:** re: the new 240-unit complex

---

Maria — quick follow-up.

One thing I didn't mention: we recently started maintaining all 8 properties for Lone Star Residential after their previous vendor no-showed on a weekend emergency.

Their portfolio is similar size to yours. Happy to share what we set up for them if it's useful — no commitment needed.

Either way, hope the Parkside transition is going smoothly.

— Jake

---

### Touch 4: Phone (Day 9)

**Voicemail script (if no answer):**
> "Hey Maria, it's Jake Torres with Apex HVAC — I've sent a couple emails about HVAC maintenance for your properties. With summer coming and Parkside onboarding, figured a quick call might save you a headache down the road. I'm at (214) 555-0172. Either way, hope you're having a good week."

*Follow up with text after voicemail:*
> Left you a quick voicemail Maria. No pressure — just figured it's worth connecting before summer hits. Let me know if there's a better time.

---

### Touch 5: Breakup Email (Day 14)

**Subject:** closing the loop

---

Maria —

I've reached out a few times and I'm guessing the timing isn't right — totally fair.

I'll close this out on my end. If HVAC headaches come up later (especially with summer around the corner), you've got my info.

Good luck with the Parkside transition.

— Jake
(214) 555-0172

---

**Cadence summary:**
| Day | Channel | Angle |
|-----|---------|-------|
| 1 | Email | New property acquisition hook |
| 3 | Text | Quick follow-up |
| 6 | Email | Case study (Lone Star Residential) |
| 9 | Phone + Text | Voicemail + text |
| 14 | Email | Breakup |

## Example 2: Marketing Agency — Reaching Restaurant Owners

### Request
> "I run a small social media agency. We specifically help restaurants get more foot traffic through Instagram and Google. I want to reach out to 3 restaurants in my area that have great food but terrible social media presence."

### Research Phase

**Lead 1: Nonna's Kitchen (Italian, family-owned, 15 years)**
- Instagram: 340 followers, last post 4 months ago
- Google: 4.7 stars, 280 reviews (people love it)
- Hook: Amazing reviews but invisible online — mismatch between food quality and digital presence
- Contact: Tony Marchetti (owner)
- Priority: Hot — strong need signal (dead social media + great reviews proves the food isn't the problem)

### Output — Single Email (Lead 1)

---

## Outreach Email: Tony Marchetti at Nonna's Kitchen

**To:** Tony Marchetti (via contact form or front desk)
**Subject:** your Google reviews are incredible
**Personalization hook:** 4.7 stars from 280 reviews vs. 340 Instagram followers — massive untapped potential

---

Tony —

I was looking up Italian restaurants in [area] and found Nonna's. Your Google reviews are unreal — 4.7 stars, people raving about the carbonara and the tiramisu.

But here's the thing: you have 340 Instagram followers. That means people are finding you by accident or word-of-mouth. Imagine what happens when they can actually SEE the food before they decide where to eat tonight.

I help restaurants turn their food into foot traffic using Instagram and Google. Nothing crazy — just making sure what's on people's screens matches what's on your plates.

Would it be worth a 10-minute conversation? No pitch, just want to show you what restaurants your size are doing that's working.

— [Your name]
[Agency name] | [phone]

P.S. — Looked at your last Instagram post (4 months ago). One carbonara video would get more reach than that post got in a year. Just saying.

---

**Why this angle:** Leading with genuine praise (the Google reviews) makes it feel like I actually care about his business. The contrast between reviews and Instagram presence is impossible to argue with — it's a fact, not a sales pitch.

**Follow-up plan:** If no reply in 3 days, drop by the restaurant for lunch. Mention the email in person. (For restaurants, in-person > digital.)

## Example 3: Accountant — Post-Meeting Follow-Up Sequence

### Request
> "I had a great meeting with a potential client yesterday — a dentist who's opening her second practice. She seemed interested but said she needs to 'think about it.' Help me follow up without being pushy."

### Context Gathered
- **Prospect:** Dr. Sarah Kim, dentist, opening second location in 2 months
- **Meeting outcome:** Interested but wants to think about it
- **Key points discussed:** Payroll for new staff, entity structure for second location, tax implications of expansion
- **Her concern:** Cost of switching from current bookkeeper + timing with everything else going on

### Output

---

## Follow-Up Sequence: Dr. Sarah Kim

**Goal:** Convert interest to engagement without pressure
**Duration:** 12 days
**Approach:** Add value, address concern indirectly, let her come to the decision

---

### Touch 1: Same-Day Recap (today, within 2 hours of meeting)

**Subject:** great talking today — quick recap

---

Sarah —

Really enjoyed our conversation today. Quick recap of what we covered:

• **Entity structure:** Separate LLC for location #2 makes sense for liability. I can handle the formation.
• **Payroll:** We'd set up payroll for the new staff before your first hire date — takes about a week.
• **Tax planning:** A few deductions specific to your expansion that we should lock in before year-end.

I know timing is a lot right now with the build-out. The good news: the entity and payroll pieces only take a few days on my end, so we'd just need to start ~3 weeks before you hire anyone.

No rush on a decision — happy to answer any other questions that come up.

— [Your name]

---

### Touch 2: Value-Add (Day 4)

**Subject:** thought of you — dental practice expansion checklist

---

Sarah —

Found this while working with another client and thought of our conversation: [link to a relevant article or checklist about dental practice expansion financial planning].

The tax timeline section on page 2 is especially relevant given your Q3 opening.

Let me know if questions come up.

— [Your name]

---

### Touch 3: Soft Close (Day 8)

**Subject:** quick question about timing

---

Sarah —

Hope the build-out is going well. Quick question:

When are you planning to make your first hire for location #2? Just want to make sure you have plenty of runway for payroll setup if you decide to work together — it's a 5-day process and I'd hate for it to hold up your opening.

No pressure either way — just flagging the timeline.

— [Your name]

---

### Touch 4: Breakup / Open Door (Day 12)

**Subject:** no worries either way

---

Sarah —

I know you've got a million things going on with the expansion. Just wanted to let you know: no pressure on my end. 

If and when the accounting/tax piece moves up your priority list, I'm here. You've got my number and I already know your situation, so we can move fast when you're ready.

Good luck with everything — exciting times.

— [Your name]

---

**Strategy notes:**
- Touch 1 addresses her concern indirectly (shows it doesn't take much time or effort on her part)
- Touch 2 gives genuine value, positions you as someone who thinks about her business even without being paid
- Touch 3 creates mild urgency around a real deadline without being pushy
- Touch 4 removes all pressure, which paradoxically often triggers action

## Example 5: Bilingual Outreach (EN/ES)

### Request
> "I need to reach out to a Mexican restaurant owner about our cleaning service. He might prefer Spanish. Can you write the email in both languages?"

### Output

---

## Outreach Email: Carlos Mendoza at El Rincón

### English Version

**Subject:** keeping El Rincón spotless without the headaches

---

Carlos —

I drive by El Rincón every week and the place always looks packed — clearly the food speaks for itself.

Quick question: how's your current cleaning setup working? I ask because a lot of restaurant owners I work with were spending too much time managing cleaning crews on top of everything else.

We handle restaurant cleaning for 4 spots in [area] — daily deep cleans, kitchen degreasing, health inspection prep. You focus on the food, we handle the mess.

Worth 5 minutes to see if we can help? Happy to swing by before your morning shift any day this week.

— [Your name]
[Company] | (555) 123-4567

---

### Spanish Version

**Subject:** mantener El Rincón impecable sin complicaciones

---

Carlos —

Paso por El Rincón cada semana y siempre está lleno — la comida habla por sí sola.

Te pregunto rápido: ¿cómo te está funcionando tu servicio de limpieza actual? Lo pregunto porque muchos dueños de restaurantes con los que trabajo estaban gastando demasiado tiempo coordinando equipos de limpieza además de todo lo demás.

Manejamos la limpieza para 4 restaurantes en [zona] — limpieza profunda diaria, desengrase de cocina, y preparación para inspecciones de salud. Tú te enfocas en la comida, nosotros nos encargamos del resto.

¿Vale la pena 5 minutos para ver si podemos ayudar? Con gusto paso antes de tu turno de la mañana cualquier día de esta semana.

— [Your name]
[Company] | (555) 123-4567

---

**Notes:**
- Spanish version uses "tú" (informal) rather than "usted" — appropriate for a fellow small business owner of similar status. If the prospect is older or more formal, switch to "usted."
- "La comida habla por sí sola" = natural idiomatic equivalent, not a word-for-word translation of the English.
- Send the Spanish version if: his website/social media is in Spanish, his Google listing is bilingual, or you're in a heavily Hispanic neighborhood. When in doubt, send English with a P.S.: "Si prefieres que hablemos en español, con gusto."
