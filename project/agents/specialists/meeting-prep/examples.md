## Example 1: First Meeting with a New Prospect

**User:** "Prep me for my meeting tomorrow with Sarah Chen from Greenfield Solutions."

**Agent gathers:**
- Calendar: Meeting at 2:00 PM, 45 minutes, video call
- CRM: New lead, came in through website form. Interested in "marketing package." No prior interactions.
- Email: One email exchange — Sarah asked about pricing, user sent a general overview.
- Web search: Sarah Chen is VP Marketing at Greenfield Solutions. Company is a 30-person SaaS
  startup in the HR tech space. Recently raised $4M seed. Sarah previously at HubSpot for 5 years.

**Agent delivers:**

```markdown
# Meeting Brief: Intro Call — Sarah Chen, Greenfield Solutions

**When:** Tomorrow, 2:00 PM (45 min, video)
**Attendees:** Sarah Chen — VP Marketing, Greenfield Solutions

---

## Why This Meeting Matters
First conversation with a qualified lead. Greenfield just raised $4M and is likely
building out their marketing function. High potential for our full marketing package.

## What You Want to Walk Out With
- Understand their current marketing setup and pain points
- Qualify budget and timeline
- Schedule a follow-up demo or proposal delivery

---

## Attendee Context

### Sarah Chen — VP Marketing, Greenfield Solutions
- **Relationship:** New — inbound lead via website form (2 days ago)
- **Background:** 5 years at HubSpot (content marketing → VP level). Joined Greenfield
  6 months ago to build marketing from scratch. (Source: LinkedIn)
- **Company:** HR tech SaaS, 30 employees, $4M seed raised 3 months ago. (Source: Crunchbase)
- **What she likely cares about:** Building a marketing engine fast with limited team.
  As ex-HubSpot, she knows good marketing — she'll be a sophisticated buyer.
- **Open items:** You sent pricing overview 2 days ago. She replied "looks interesting,
  let's chat." No specific questions yet.

---

## Suggested Agenda
1. Introductions + what prompted her to reach out (5 min)
2. Current marketing setup and biggest gaps (15 min)
3. Walk through how we've helped similar companies (10 min)
4. Budget/timeline/decision process (10 min)
5. Next steps (5 min)

## Talking Points
- "Congrats on the raise — sounds like an exciting time to be building at Greenfield"
- "With your HubSpot background, you probably have strong opinions on what good
  marketing ops looks like. I'd love to hear what you're envisioning."
- "We've worked with 3 other HR tech startups post-seed — happy to share what worked"
- Ask: "Is it just you on the marketing team right now, or are you hiring?"

## Watch For
- She may be price-sensitive (seed-stage budget) — be ready to discuss phased approach
- Ex-HubSpot means she may want to do things in-house — position as augmentation, not replacement
- Check: Is she the decision maker or does a CEO/co-founder need to sign off?

---

## Background Materials
- [Email thread: pricing overview sent 7/14]
- [Greenfield Solutions website: greenfieldsolutions.com]
```

## Example 2: Recurring Weekly 1:1 (Quick Mode)

**User:** "Quick prep for my 1:1 with Marcus at 3."

**Agent gathers:**
- Calendar: Recurring weekly 1:1 with Marcus Reeves (direct report), 30 min
- Previous meeting notes: Last week discussed Q3 planning and delayed client deliverable
- Email: Marcus sent a thread about the Henderson account needing attention
- CRM: Henderson account health score dropped to "at risk"

**Agent delivers (Quick mode):**

```markdown
# Quick Prep: 1:1 with Marcus Reeves

**When:** Today, 3:00 PM (30 min, recurring)

## Carry-Forward from Last Week
- [ ] Marcus was going to finalize Q3 plan by Friday — check if done
- [ ] Client deliverable for Apex was delayed — status?

## New Since Last Week
- Marcus flagged Henderson account as needing attention (email, Tuesday)
- Henderson health score dropped to "at risk" in CRM
- Good opening: "How's the Henderson situation? I saw it flagged."

## Suggested Focus
1. Henderson account — what's happening, what support does Marcus need?
2. Q3 plan — done? Any blockers?
3. Apex deliverable — resolved or still slipping?
```

## Example 3: Board Meeting Prep (Deep Research)

**User:** "I have a board meeting next Thursday. Help me prep."

**Agent gathers:**
- Calendar: Board meeting, 2 hours, in-person. Attendees: 3 board members + user
- Previous board notes: Last quarter's minutes, action items, decisions
- Email: Pre-read materials sent by board chair last week
- Documents: Last quarter's financials, updated cap table, product roadmap
- Web: Board member profiles, recent investments, public statements

**Agent delivers:**

```markdown
# Meeting Brief: Q3 Board Meeting

**When:** Thursday, 10:00 AM - 12:00 PM (in-person, conference room)
**Attendees:** Jennifer Walsh (Chair), David Okafor, Priya Patel, You

---

## Why This Meeting Matters
Quarterly governance meeting. Key decisions: approve Q4 budget and discuss
Series A timeline. Jennifer's email suggests she wants to push timeline up.

## What You Want to Walk Out With
- Board approval on Q4 budget (including new hire)
- Alignment on Series A timing — you prefer Q1 next year, Jennifer may push for Q4
- Updated action items for next quarter

---

## Attendee Context

### Jennifer Walsh — Board Chair
- **Background:** Managing Partner at Walsh Ventures. Led your seed round.
- **Last interaction:** Email 5 days ago — sent pre-read materials and noted
  "I want to discuss accelerating the raise." (Source: email)
- **What she cares about:** Growth metrics, burn rate, fundraising timing.
- **Style note:** Prefers data-driven arguments. Bring specific numbers.

### David Okafor — Board Member
- **Background:** Former CEO of TechScale (acquired 2022). Angel investor.
- **Last interaction:** Board meeting last quarter. Was supportive of hiring plan.
- **What he cares about:** Team building, operational efficiency.
- **Recent news:** Just joined advisory board of CompetitorX. (Source: LinkedIn, 2 weeks ago)
  — Worth noting but may not be relevant.

### Priya Patel — Board Member
- **Background:** Partner at Horizon Capital. Joined board 6 months ago.
- **Last interaction:** 1:1 coffee last month. Expressed concern about CAC trending up.
- **What she cares about:** Unit economics, path to profitability.
- **Open items:** You promised her a revised CAC analysis — did you send it?

---

## Suggested Agenda
1. CEO update + key metrics (15 min)
2. Q4 budget presentation + discussion (30 min) — **decision needed**
3. Series A timing discussion (30 min) — **alignment needed**
4. Product roadmap update (15 min)
5. Action items + close (10 min)

## Talking Points
- On Series A timing: Come prepared with both scenarios (Q4 vs Q1). Have
  metrics that support your preferred timeline.
- For Priya: Lead with CAC improvement data if available — address her concern proactively.
- For David: Acknowledge the CompetitorX news casually. Don't make it weird.
- Budget: Frame the new hire as essential to hitting the metrics that make Series A viable.

## Watch For
- Jennifer may try to push fundraising timeline. Have your "why Q1 is better" argument tight.
- Priya will ask about unit economics. Have CAC/LTV numbers ready.
- David's CompetitorX advisory role — any conflict of interest? Probably fine but be aware.
- Check: Did you send Priya the CAC analysis you promised? If not, send before the meeting.

---

## Background Materials
- [Pre-read materials from Jennifer (email, 7/10)]
- [Last quarter's board minutes]
- [Q4 budget draft]
- [Product roadmap v3]
- [CAC analysis for Priya — STATUS: check if sent]
```

## Example 4: No Data Available (Graceful Degradation)

**User:** "Prep me for my lunch with Tom Reynolds tomorrow."

**Agent gathers:**
- Calendar: "Lunch - Tom" at 12:30, no other details. No location.
- CRM: No record for "Tom Reynolds"
- Email: No threads with anyone named Tom Reynolds in last 90 days
- Web search: Multiple "Tom Reynolds" — can't determine which one without more context

**Agent delivers:**

```markdown
# Meeting Brief: Lunch with Tom Reynolds

**When:** Tomorrow, 12:30 PM
**Where:** Not specified — do you know where you're meeting?

---

## Context Gap

I wasn't able to find context on Tom Reynolds:
- No CRM record
- No email history
- Multiple results on LinkedIn — I'm not sure which Tom Reynolds this is

**Can you help me narrow it down?**
- How do you know Tom? (Client, friend, referral, etc.)
- What company is he at?
- What's the purpose of the lunch?

With even one of these, I can likely pull together a proper brief.

---

## In the Meantime — Generic Prep

Since this appears to be an informal lunch:

### General Talking Points
- What prompted the meeting? (referral, reconnection, business opportunity?)
- Listen first — if you're not sure of the purpose, ask "What made you want to get together?"
- Have your 30-second "what I'm working on" answer ready

### Logistics
- [ ] Confirm location/restaurant
- [ ] Confirm time still works (send a quick "Looking forward to tomorrow!")
```

## Key Patterns Across Examples

1. **Always lead with "Why This Meeting Matters" and "What You Want"** — the principal
   should know the stakes and goal within 5 seconds of reading.

2. **Calibrate depth to meeting importance** — A weekly 1:1 gets 5 lines. A board meeting
   gets a full page. Don't over-prepare for casual meetings.

3. **Flag risks and open items prominently** — If there's something the principal promised
   and hasn't delivered, surface it with a ⚠️. Better to know before the meeting.

4. **Graceful degradation is acceptable** — When you can't find info, say so clearly and
   ask targeted questions to fill the gap. A partial brief is still useful.

5. **End with next steps** — Every brief either offers to go deeper or suggests a post-meeting
   action (capture notes, send follow-up, schedule next meeting).
