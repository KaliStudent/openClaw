# Competitive Intelligence — Reference

Detailed research methodology, source evaluation protocols, and analysis frameworks for the Competitive Intelligence agent.

---

## Research Methodology

### Phase 1: Web Search Strategy

For each competitor, execute searches in this order:

**Identity searches (who are they):**
1. `"[competitor name]"` — homepage, about page
2. `"[competitor name]" [city/area]` — local presence confirmation
3. `site:[competitor-domain.com]` — full site index

**Service/Pricing searches:**
4. `"[competitor name]" pricing OR prices OR cost OR rates`
5. `"[competitor name]" services OR "what we offer"`
6. `"[competitor name]" [specific service you compete on]`

**Reputation searches:**
7. `"[competitor name]" reviews` — Google, Yelp, industry sites
8. `"[competitor name]" complaints OR problems`
9. `"[competitor name]" vs` — comparison content others have written

**Signal searches:**
10. `"[competitor name]" hiring OR careers OR jobs`
11. `"[competitor name]" news OR announced OR launched`
12. `"[competitor name]" [year]` — recent mentions

**Customer searches:**
13. `"[competitor name]" customer OR client OR testimonial`
14. `"[competitor name]" case study OR results`

### Phase 2: Review Analysis Protocol

**Where to look for reviews:**
| Business Type | Primary Review Sites |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Local services | Google Business, Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor |
| Restaurants | Google, Yelp, TripAdvisor, DoorDash/Uber Eats |
| Medical/Dental | Google, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Vitals |
| Legal | Google, Avvo, Martindale |
| SaaS/Tech | G2, Capterra, TrustRadius |
| Financial | Google, BBB, NerdWallet |

**Review analysis framework:**

Read 20-30 reviews minimum (if available). Categorize into:

1. **Themes in 5-star reviews** — What do fans consistently praise?
2. **Themes in 1-2 star reviews** — What do critics consistently complain about?
3. **Recurring keywords** — Words that appear in multiple reviews
4. **Response patterns** — How does the business respond to negative reviews? (Tells you about their customer service culture)

**Extract:**
- Top 3 praise themes (these are their real strengths)
- Top 3 complaint themes (these are their real weaknesses)
- Any patterns in WHEN bad experiences happen (busy seasons, specific services, staffing)
- Whether complaints are about quality, communication, pricing, or reliability

### Phase 3: Pricing Intelligence

**Direct pricing sources (highest confidence 🟢):**
- Their website pricing page
- Menu/rate cards available online
- Third-party aggregators (insurance panels, marketplace listings)

**Indirect pricing signals (medium confidence 🟡):**
- Forum discussions where customers share prices
- Competitor comparison sites
- Job postings with compensation (infer margins)
- News articles mentioning project values

**Inference-based (low confidence 🔴):**
- Industry averages applied to their market segment
- Competitor's target market suggests price range
- Their marketing language ("premium" vs "affordable")

**Pricing documentation rules:**
- Always note the date pricing was observed
- Note whether it's "starting at" vs actual typical price
- Note what's included vs. extra (the real comparison)
- Flag if pricing requires a call/quote (can't be verified without calling)

## Source Evaluation Framework

### Credibility Assessment

| Source Type | Credibility Level | Why |
|-------------|-------------------|-----|
| Their own website | High (for claims they make) | They control it — it's what they SAY |
| Google Reviews (verified) | High | Google verifies purchase/visit |
| News from reputable outlet | High | Journalistic standards |
| Industry review sites | Medium-High | Usually verified buyers |
| Social media posts | Medium | Could be curated/biased |
| Forum discussions | Medium-Low | Anonymous, may be outdated |
| Single review/comment | Low | Could be outlier or fake |
| Competitor comparison by third party | Variable | Check who funded the comparison |

### Cross-Referencing Rules

- **Single source claim:** Marked 🟡 at best (even if credible source)
- **Two independent sources agree:** Can be marked 🟢
- **Sources conflict:** Present BOTH, note conflict, mark highest confidence as 🟡
- **Information older than 6 months:** Automatically downgrade by one level
- **Information from the competitor themselves:** High confidence for what they claim, but note it's self-reported

### Handling Uncertain Information

When you can't fully verify something:

```
✅ DO: "[Competitor] appears to charge around $X based on their website pricing page
(last updated [date]). Note: this is for their basic package; project-based pricing 
may differ. Confidence: 🟡"

❌ DON'T: "[Competitor] charges $X." (No source, no caveat, presented as certain)
```

## Analysis Frameworks

### Porter's Five Forces (Simplified for Small Business)

For market positioning, assess:

1. **Direct Competitors** — Who does the same thing for the same customers?
2. **Substitute Threats** — What else could customers do instead? (DIY, different solution type)
3. **New Entrants** — Who's entering this market? (New franchises, online alternatives)
4. **Customer Power** — How easy is it for customers to switch? What gives them leverage?
5. **Supplier Power** — What inputs does the business depend on? (Labor, materials, software)

### Competitive Advantage Types

| Type | Description | Example (Local Plumber) |
|------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Cost** | Same service, lower price | "We're 20% below market rates" |
| **Speed** | Same service, faster delivery | "Same-day emergency service" |
| **Quality** | Better outcome or experience | "Licensed master plumbers only" |
| **Specialization** | Narrower focus = deeper expertise | "We only do repiping — not everything" |
| **Relationship** | Trust and familiarity | "Serving this neighborhood for 30 years" |
| **Convenience** | Easier to work with | "Online booking, real-time arrival tracking" |
| **Breadth** | One-stop shop | "Full home services: plumbing, HVAC, electrical" |

### Win/Loss Pattern Analysis

When CRM data is available, look for patterns:

**Wins analysis:**
- What type of customer do you win?
- What stage of need? (Urgent vs. planned)
- What did they cite as the reason?
- What competitor were they considering?
- What was the deal size pattern?

**Loss analysis:**
- What type of customer do you lose?
- At what stage in the process do you lose them?
- What reason did they give?
- Who did they go with?
- Was it price, timing, capability, or something else?

**Pattern template:**
```
We TEND TO WIN when:
- Customer is [type]
- Need is [urgent/planned/specific]
- They value [attribute]
- Deal size is [$range]

We TEND TO LOSE when:
- Customer is [type]
- They prioritize [attribute over our strength]
- Competitor is [who]
- Deal size is [$range]
```

## Battlecard Construction

### Structure Rules

A battlecard must be usable in a live conversation — it needs to be:
1. **Scannable** — Find any answer in under 5 seconds
2. **Quotable** — Talking points are ready to say out loud
3. **Honest** — Acknowledges competitor strengths (builds trust with the prospect)
4. **Specific** — Numbers, names, facts — not generalities
5. **Updated** — Date-stamped, with known freshness of data

### Talking Point Rules

**For each competitor scenario, provide a response that:**
1. Acknowledges the competitor (never badmouths)
2. Bridges to your differentiator
3. Offers proof (a specific customer story, number, or fact)
4. Ends with a question that advances the conversation

**Example framework:**
```
"[Competitor] is a solid company — they're known for [genuine strength].
Where we tend to differ is [your specific differentiator].
For example, [specific proof point with customer name/result].
What matters most to you: [their strength] or [your strength]?"
```

### Landmine Questions

Landmine questions are ethical — they're real questions about real needs that happen to highlight areas where you're stronger. They are NOT:
- Trick questions
- Misleading framings
- Gotcha moments
- Badmouthing disguised as questions

**Good landmine questions:**
- "How important is [thing you do well that they don't] to you?"
- "What happens when [scenario where competitor typically fails]?"
- "Have you asked [competitor] about [thing they charge extra for]?"
- "What's your experience been with [their known weakness area]?"

**Rules:**
- Only create landmines based on verified weaknesses (🟢 or 🟡 confidence)
- The question must be relevant even if the competitor weren't in the picture
- Never suggest something untrue about the competitor — just highlight genuine gaps

## Competitive Monitoring

### What to Track Over Time

Set up ongoing monitoring for:
| Signal | What It Means | Where to Watch |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Price change | Strategy shift, pressure | Their website, marketplace listings |
| New service/product | Expansion into your territory | Their website, social media, press |
| Hiring surge | Growth or new initiative | LinkedIn, job boards |
| Leadership change | Strategy shift coming | LinkedIn, news |
| Negative review spike | Quality or staffing issues | Google, Yelp |
| New location/market | Expansion = competition | News, their website |
| Marketing campaign | New positioning or offer | Social media, ads |
| Partnership announcement | New capability or reach | Press, social |

### Refresh Cadence

| Competitor Type | Refresh Frequency | What to Update |
|-----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Primary (top 2-3) | Monthly | Full profile refresh |
| Secondary (next 3-5) | Quarterly | News and pricing check |
| Emerging (new entrants) | When detected | Initial profile, then quarterly |

### Alert Triggers (When to Proactively Report)

Notify the business owner immediately when:
- Competitor changes pricing (especially if they lower it)
- Competitor launches a new service that directly competes with you
- Competitor gets a wave of negative reviews (opportunity)
- Competitor appears to be closing/struggling (opportunity)
- New competitor enters the market
- Competitor wins a deal you were pursuing

## Industry-Specific Research Notes

### Local Services (Plumbing, HVAC, Electrical, etc.)
- Check: Licensing, insurance, BBB rating
- Compare: Response time, warranty, pricing transparency
- Look for: Complaints about no-shows, upselling, hidden fees
- Key differentiator opportunities: Speed, pricing transparency, warranty

### Medical/Dental
- Check: Provider credentials, specialties, patient reviews
- Compare: Wait times, bedside manner, modern equipment, insurance acceptance
- Look for: Complaints about billing, wait times, dismissive staff
- Key differentiator opportunities: Patient experience, communication, technology

### Professional Services (Legal, Accounting, Marketing)
- Check: Credentials, specializations, notable clients
- Compare: Responsiveness, pricing model (hourly vs. fixed), expertise depth
- Look for: Complaints about communication, surprise bills, missed deadlines
- Key differentiator opportunities: Communication, pricing transparency, specialization

### Restaurants/Food Service
- Check: Menu, pricing, health scores, delivery platforms
- Compare: Food quality, ambiance, service speed, value
- Look for: Complaints about service, consistency, cleanliness
- Key differentiator opportunities: Uniqueness, consistency, community connection

### Real Estate
- Check: Listings, transaction volume, areas served, specialties
- Compare: Response time, marketing quality, commission structure
- Look for: Complaints about communication, availability, follow-through
- Key differentiator opportunities: Local expertise, responsiveness, technology use

## Ethical Guidelines

### What We Do
- Research publicly available information
- Analyze real customer feedback
- Present findings honestly (including competitor strengths)
- Cite sources for all claims
- Make clear recommendations based on evidence

### What We Don't Do
- Misrepresent competitor offerings
- Use confidential or proprietary competitor information
- Encourage prospects to break contracts unethically
- Fabricate negative claims about competitors
- Pose as customers to gather intelligence (recommend if needed, but flag it)
- Encourage badmouthing in sales conversations

### The Honesty Standard

> "If a competitor's salesperson read our battlecard about them, they should be able to
> say 'that's tough but fair' — not 'that's a lie.'"

This standard ensures our competitive intelligence builds trust with prospects (they can
tell when you're being honest vs. being a mudslinger) and protects the business's reputation.
