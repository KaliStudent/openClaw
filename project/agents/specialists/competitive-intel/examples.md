## Example 2: Landscaping Company — Quick Battlecard

### Request
> "I keep running into GreenScape Pros when bidding on commercial properties. Need a quick battlecard."

### Output

---

## Battlecard: You vs. GreenScape Pros

### 30-Second Summary
GreenScape is a volume operation — they manage 50+ commercial properties with lower per-property pricing but standardized service. You're the premium option: fewer clients, more attention, custom design work. You win when the property manager cares about curb appeal and tenant satisfaction. They win when budget is the #1 factor.

### When You Win
- Property is high-visibility (retail centers, medical offices, Class A office)
- Manager has been burned by "cheap" landscaping that let things die
- Custom design/seasonal rotation is important
- They want a single point of contact who knows their property
- Property has complex irrigation or specific plant requirements

### When They Win
- Pure budget decision (they're typically 15-25% cheaper)
- Large portfolio needing uniform service across many properties
- Basic mow-and-blow is sufficient
- Manager doesn't differentiate between landscaping providers

### Key Differentiators

| Factor | You | GreenScape |
|--------|-----|------------|
| Crew consistency | Same crew every visit | Rotating crews based on schedule |
| Account management | Owner-operated, direct line | Account rep (may change) |
| Custom design | In-house landscape architect | Template planting plans |
| Response time | Same-day for emergencies | 24-48 hour SLA |
| Seasonal rotations | Included in maintenance contract | Billed separately |
| Client load | ~15 commercial accounts | 50+ commercial accounts |

### Kill Shots (Honest Questions)

1. "How important is it that the same crew works your property every time?" — Exposes: their rotating crew model means nobody truly knows the property
2. "Have you had issues with plants dying or looking thin by mid-season?" — Highlights: your horticultural expertise vs. their mow-and-blow approach
3. "When something goes wrong on a Saturday, how quickly do you need a response?" — Reveals: your same-day vs. their 24-48 hour SLA

### Objection Handling

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "GreenScape is cheaper" | "They usually are — about 20%. The question is whether saving $300/month is worth it if your tenants start complaining about dead plants in August. Our properties look great year-round because we have a horticulturist managing plant health, not just a mowing schedule." |
| "GreenScape manages 50+ properties" | "That's their model — volume. Ours is depth. I personally know your property's irrigation zones, which beds get afternoon shade, and which varieties survive your parking lot's reflected heat. That knowledge keeps things alive." |

**Confidence:** 🟢 (based on their website, Google Reviews, and field experience competing against them)

3. The specialty commands premium pricing — restaurant owners will pay more for someone who catches COGS drift and labor compliance issues before they become fines

## Example 4: Home Remodeler — Win/Loss Analysis

### Request
> "I've lost 3 bids this quarter to the same company — Precision Renovations. Help me figure out why and what to do."

### Research + Analysis

**Data gathered from user:**
- Lost bid 1: Kitchen remodel, $45K → Client chose Precision ($41K)
- Lost bid 2: Bathroom + master suite, $62K → Client chose Precision ($58K)
- Lost bid 3: Full home renovation, $180K → Client chose Precision ($165K)

**Pattern identified:** Price difference is 8-10% consistently. But is it really price?

### Output

---

## Win/Loss Analysis: You vs. Precision Renovations

### The Pattern

| Deal | Your Bid | Their Bid | Difference | Stated Reason |
|------|----------|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Kitchen remodel | $45,000 | $41,000 | -9.7% | "Better price" |
| Bath + master | $62,000 | $58,000 | -6.9% | "More competitive" |
| Full reno | $180,000 | $165,000 | -8.3% | "Budget constraints" |

**Surface diagnosis:** You're losing on price (8-10% higher consistently).

**Deeper analysis:** Let me look at what else might be happening...

---

### Precision Renovations Research

| Finding | Evidence | Confidence |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Aggressive pricing to build market share (newer company) | Founded 2023, growing fast, 8 Google reviews | 🟡 |
| Uses subcontractors for most trades | Job postings only for project managers, not tradespeople | 🟡 |
| 3D rendering in proposals | Mentioned in 2 positive reviews: "loved seeing the design before starting" | 🟢 |
| Mixed quality reviews | 4.2 stars (vs. typical established remodelers at 4.6+) | 🟢 |
| Change order complaints | 2 reviews mention "final price was higher than quoted" | 🟡 |

---

### Why You're Really Losing

It might not be price alone. Consider:

1. **Presentation gap** — Their proposals include 3D renderings. Yours may be text-heavy bids. The rendering makes the lower price feel LESS risky because the client can "see" what they're getting.

2. **Anchoring effect** — If they present first, their lower number anchors the client's expectation. Your higher bid then feels expensive even if it includes more.

3. **"Good enough" perception** — For clients who don't know construction, 8% savings on an already-expensive project feels huge. They can't SEE the quality difference until it's built.

---

### Recommended Counter-Strategy

**Don't lower your price.** Here's why: their 2 change order complaints suggest their "lower" bid becomes similar or higher by project end. Your honest bidding is a feature, not a bug.

**Instead:**

1. **Add visual proposals** — Even basic SketchUp renderings or curated inspiration boards close the presentation gap. The visual isn't about art — it's about reducing perceived risk.

2. **Itemize your bids** — Show exactly what's included. When a client sees your line items, they can compare apples-to-apples. Precision's lower bid may exclude things you include.

3. **Introduce the "final price" conversation** — In your proposal meeting, say: *"One thing to ask any contractor: what percentage of your projects come in at or below the quoted price? Ours is 94%."* This plants the seed without badmouthing.

4. **Ask the question earlier** — Before bidding: *"Are you evaluating other contractors? What's most important to you beyond price?"* If price is truly #1, you may choose not to bid (and save yourself the time).

5. **Warranty and guarantee** — Do you offer a written workmanship guarantee they don't? Make it prominent.

---

### Landmine Questions for Bid Presentations

Ask prospects these questions during your proposal meeting:

1. "Has anyone quoted you a breakdown of what's included vs. what's additional? I find that's where final costs diverge." — Exposes potential hidden costs
2. "How important is it that the same team works your project start to finish?" — Highlights your in-house crews vs. their rotating subs
3. "Have you seen examples of each contractor's completed work in person?" — Your portfolio is likely stronger (they're newer)

### Request (Proactive Alert)
> "Hey, wanted to flag something — your competitor Digital First just posted that they're launching a 'social media in a box' package for $499/month. Thought you should know."

### Output

---

## ⚡ Competitive Alert: Digital First — New Package Launch

**Detected:** July 2026
**Source:** Digital First Instagram post + website update
**Confidence:** 🟢 (primary source — their own announcement)

---

### What They Announced

Digital First launched "Social in a Box" — a productized social media management package:
- 12 posts/month across 2 platforms
- Basic graphic design (templates)
- Monthly analytics report
- No strategy, no ad management, no custom content
- Price: $499/month, no contract

**Source:** digitalfirstagency.com/social-in-a-box (posted July 14, 2026)

---

### What This Means for You

**Threat level:** Medium

**Why it matters:**
- $499 significantly undercuts your custom packages
- They're targeting the same small business market
- Productized = scalable; they can serve more clients at this price

**Why it might NOT matter:**
- 12 posts/month across 2 platforms is very thin (that's 3 posts/week/platform)
- "Template" design means generic, not brand-specific
- No strategy means no ROI thinking — just post-and-pray
- No contract means high churn (clients will leave when they don't see results)

---

### Recommended Response

**Don't panic. Don't lower prices.** This is a volume play that will attract price-sensitive clients who would've gone DIY anyway.

**Do this instead:**

1. **Clarify your positioning:** "Custom strategy vs. template posts" — make the distinction clear on your website and in sales conversations.

2. **Create a comparison** (for your own sales use, not public):
   - Their 12 templates/month vs. your custom-researched content
   - Their "report" vs. your strategic recommendations
   - Their "no strategy" vs. your ROI-driven approach

3. **Consider a starter tier** (optional): If you're losing too many price-sensitive leads, a $799-999 "essentials" package with limited scope could capture the middle market without cannibalizing your premium offering.

4. **Watch their results:** In 3-6 months, check if their clients are seeing results or churning. Template approaches often fail for local businesses because they lack personalization.
