# Content Creator — Reference

Detailed rules, platform specifications, and operational protocols for the Content Creator agent.

## Brand Voice Deep Dive

### Voice Extraction Process

When a business provides sample content, analyze these dimensions:

1. **Formality Level** (1-5 scale)
   - 1: Very casual ("hey y'all, check this out")
   - 3: Conversational professional ("Here's what we learned")
   - 5: Formal ("We are pleased to announce")

2. **Humor Usage**
   - None: Straight informational
   - Light: Occasional wit, wordplay
   - Heavy: Memes, jokes, self-deprecating humor

3. **Technical Depth**
   - Surface: Benefits-focused, no jargon
   - Mixed: Some industry terms, explained naturally
   - Deep: Assumes audience knows the field

4. **Emotional Tone**
   - Empathetic: "We know how frustrating this is"
   - Confident: "Here's exactly how to fix it"
   - Aspirational: "Imagine what your home could look like"

5. **Sentence Structure**
   - Short and punchy (trades, restaurants, gyms)
   - Medium and flowing (coaches, consultants)
   - Varied with storytelling (agencies, luxury services)

### Voice Consistency Rules

- Once a voice profile is established, NEVER break character
- If asked to write something that conflicts with the voice, flag it: "This feels different from your usual tone — want me to adjust it to match, or is this intentionally different?"
- Voice applies across ALL channels, with platform-appropriate adjustments to length and format (but not personality)

### Industry Voice Defaults

When no samples are provided, use these starting points:

| Industry | Default Voice | Example Phrase |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| Plumbing/HVAC/Trades | Straight-talking expert | "Here's what's actually going on with your water heater" |
| Dental/Medical | Warm, reassuring professional | "We know the dentist isn't everyone's favorite place" |
| Restaurant/Cafe | Warm, inviting, food-loving | "You know that feeling when the queso hits just right?" |
| Law firm | Knowledgeable, accessible | "Let's break down what this actually means for you" |
| Real estate | Enthusiastic, local expert | "This neighborhood is having a moment" |
| Fitness/Gym | Motivating, no-BS | "Skip the shortcuts. Here's what actually works" |
| Salon/Spa | Friendly, confidence-building | "That fresh color feeling? Unmatched" |
| Auto repair | Honest, educational | "Before you panic about that check engine light..." |

## Platform Algorithm Considerations

### Instagram (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
- Reels: 15-30 seconds optimal, hook in first 2 seconds
- Carousel posts: high save rate, 7-10 slides optimal
- Comments and shares weigh more than likes
- Posting 4-7 times per week is the sweet spot
- Stories don't affect feed algorithm but drive DMs

**Formatting rules:**
- Caption limit: 2,200 characters
- First 125 characters show before "more" — make them count
- Line breaks: use periods or dashes on empty lines to force spacing
- Hashtag strategy: 5-15 tags, mix sizes (50K-500K post tags perform best)
- Alt text: always fill in for accessibility + SEO

**Content types ranked by reach:**
1. Reels (highest organic reach)
2. Carousel posts
3. Single image posts
4. Stories (lowest reach but highest engagement per view)

### Facebook (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
- Native video (especially live)
- Posts that generate meaningful comments (not "nice!")
- Group posts over page posts
- Content that keeps people on Facebook (no external links)

**Formatting rules:**
- Optimal post length: 40-80 characters for engagement
- Long-form posts: fine but need strong hooks
- Video: 1-3 minutes optimal, square or vertical
- Avoid engagement bait ("tag a friend who…")
- Links: put in comments if possible, or accept lower reach

**Local business tips:**
- Check-ins, reviews, and local tags boost visibility
- Facebook Events for anything in-person
- Messenger response time affects page ranking
- Community groups > business pages for organic reach

### LinkedIn (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
- Dwell time (people stopping to read)
- Comments in first hour
- Document (PDF carousel) posts
- Personal stories with professional lessons
- Native content (no external links in first post)

**Formatting rules:**
- Hook in first 2 lines (before "see more")
- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences)
- Use line breaks liberally
- Emojis: 1-3 per post, professional ones (✅ 💡 📊)
- Hashtags: 3-5 at the bottom only
- Character limit: 3,000 (aim for 1,200-1,500 for optimal engagement)

**What works for small businesses:**
- Founder stories (why you started)
- Behind-the-scenes of the work
- Lessons learned / mistakes made
- Client results (with permission)
- Industry education

### Twitter/X (2024-2025)

**What the algorithm rewards:**
- Replies and quote tweets
- Image/video posts over text-only
- Threads (especially with a strong Thread 1)
- Bookmarks (signal of value)

**Formatting rules:**
- 280 character limit
- Threads: number each tweet, hook the first one hard
- Images: 1600x900 for link cards, 1200x675 for images
- Video: 2:20 max, 30-60 seconds optimal
- No more than 2 hashtags per tweet

### Email Best Practices

**Subject line formulas:**
- Question: "Still dealing with [pain point]?"
- Number: "3 things about [topic] we learned this month"
- Curiosity gap: "We almost didn't share this..."
- Direct benefit: "Save 2 hours on [task] this week"
- Personal: "Quick question, [Name]"

**Send time optimization:**
- B2B: Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11 AM recipient's timezone
- B2C local: Tuesday or Thursday, 7-9 AM or 5-7 PM
- Newsletters: Consistent day/time builds habit
- Promotions: Based on purchase patterns

**Email structure rules:**
- 200-300 words for promotional emails
- 500-800 words for newsletters
- Single-column layout (mobile-first)
- One primary CTA above the fold
- Secondary CTA after main content
- P.S. line for urgency or secondary offer

## Content Calendar Strategy

### Monthly Calendar Framework

**Content pillars (choose 3-5 per business):**
1. Educational (teach something useful)
2. Behind-the-scenes (build trust/connection)
3. Social proof (reviews, results, testimonials)
4. Promotional (offers, services, CTAs)
5. Community/Culture (local events, team, values)
6. Entertainment (humor, trends, relatable moments)

**Distribution rule:** No more than 20% promotional. The rest earns the right to sell.

**Weekly rhythm example (5 posts/week):**
- Monday: Educational tip
- Tuesday: Behind-the-scenes
- Wednesday: Client result/testimonial
- Thursday: Industry trend or commentary
- Friday: Fun/personality + soft CTA

### Seasonal Content Planning

Always plan content around:
- Industry-specific seasons (tax season, wedding season, back-to-school)
- Local events and holidays
- Business milestones (anniversaries, new hires)
- National awareness days (relevant ones only)
- Weather and seasonal changes that affect the business

### Content Batching Protocol

When building a week/month of content:
1. Identify the content pillars for this business
2. Map the posting frequency per platform
3. Create theme for each slot (pillar + platform)
4. Write ALL content in one batch (efficiency)
5. Include visual direction for each piece
6. Note which pieces should be cross-posted vs. platform-unique

## SEO Deep Dive

### Keyword Research Protocol

When writing blog content:
1. Ask about the business's target keywords (if they know them)
2. If they don't know: identify 3-5 "money keywords" based on their services
3. Find long-tail variants (questions people ask)
4. Check "People Also Ask" boxes for related topics
5. Target one primary keyword per post + 2-3 secondary

### Blog SEO Checklist

Every blog post must include:
- [ ] Primary keyword in title (naturally)
- [ ] Primary keyword in first 100 words
- [ ] Primary keyword in one H2 subheading
- [ ] Secondary keywords in other H2/H3 headings
- [ ] Meta title under 60 characters
- [ ] Meta description under 160 characters (includes keyword, compels click)
- [ ] URL slug: short, descriptive, includes keyword
- [ ] At least 2 internal links to other pages on their site
- [ ] At least 1 external link to authoritative source
- [ ] Image alt text with keyword where natural
- [ ] Content length: minimum 800 words (1,500+ for competitive topics)

### Local SEO for Blog Content

For local businesses, every blog post should:
- Include city/neighborhood name naturally (1-2 times)
- Reference local landmarks, events, or context where relevant
- Use "near me" variants in headings where natural
- Link to Google Business Profile and location pages

## Repurposing Protocol

### One Piece → Many Posts

When repurposing content:

**Blog → Social:**
- Pull 3-5 key quotes or stats for individual posts
- Create a "thread" version for Twitter
- Extract a listicle for a carousel post
- Write a "teaser" post that links to the full article

**Email → Social:**
- Adapt the subject line as a hook
- Pull the key insight for a standalone post
- Use the CTA as a story with poll/question

**Video/Podcast → Written:**
- Transcribe key moments for quote graphics
- Write a summary blog post
- Create an audiogram clip (suggest 30-60 seconds)
- Pull Q&A for a FAQ post

**Customer Interaction → Content:**
- Common questions become educational posts
- Great reviews become testimonial graphics
- Before/after results become case study posts
- Funny moments become relatable content

### Repurposing Rules

- NEVER just copy/paste across platforms — always adapt
- Platform-native formatting on each
- Stagger timing (don't post everywhere simultaneously)
- Each platform version should feel intentional, not lazy

## Email Campaign Frameworks

### Welcome Sequence (New Subscriber)

**Email 1 (Immediately):** Welcome + deliver promised value (lead magnet, discount, etc.)
**Email 2 (Day 2):** Introduce yourself/business, share your story
**Email 3 (Day 4):** Provide value — tip, insight, resource
**Email 4 (Day 7):** Social proof — customer story or results
**Email 5 (Day 10):** Soft CTA — invite to take next step

### Re-engagement Sequence (Cold Subscribers)

**Email 1:** "We miss you" + strongest recent content
**Email 2 (Day 3):** Special offer for returning
**Email 3 (Day 7):** "Last chance" or "Should we stop emailing you?"

### Promotional Campaign (Sale/Launch)

**Email 1 (Day -3):** Teaser — something's coming
**Email 2 (Day 0):** Launch announcement + main CTA
**Email 3 (Day 2):** Social proof + urgency
**Email 4 (Day 5):** Last chance + scarcity

## Content Quality Standards

### The "Would I Stop Scrolling?" Test

Before delivering any social post, ask:
1. Does the first line make you want to read more?
2. Is this saying something specific (not generic platitudes)?
3. Would a real person share this with a friend?
4. Does it have a clear next action for the reader?

If any answer is "no," rewrite.

### The "So What?" Test

For every claim or statement in content:
- "We've been in business for 20 years" → So what? → "20 years means we've seen every weird pipe issue your house can throw at us"
- "We use the latest technology" → So what? → "Digital X-rays mean 80% less radiation and you see your results in real time"

### Avoiding AI-Sounding Content

Never use these phrases:
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Are you tired of...?"
- "Look no further!"
- "Game-changing"
- "Unlock your potential"
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y"
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Dive deep"
- "Seamlessly"

Instead: Write like the business owner talks to a customer face-to-face.

## Confirmation Protocol

### Before Publishing Externally

If connected to scheduling/sending tools:
1. **Present** the final content with all metadata
2. **Confirm** — "Ready to schedule this for [date/time]?"
3. **Wait** for explicit approval before scheduling or sending
4. **Confirm** delivery: "Posted to [platform] at [time]"

### Batch Approval

For calendar/batch content:
1. Present all content pieces together
2. Ask: "Approve all, or want to review individually?"
3. Allow edits to specific pieces
4. Schedule approved pieces, hold flagged ones

## Edge Cases

### Content About Competitors
- Never mention competitors by name in social content
- For blog posts: comparison content is fine if factual and fair
- Always lead with own strengths, not competitor weaknesses
- "Unlike other [industry] businesses" is acceptable; naming them is not

### Regulated Industries
- Medical/dental: No specific health claims without "consult your provider"
- Legal: "This is general information, not legal advice"
- Financial: No guarantees of returns or outcomes
- Real estate: Fair housing compliance in all content

### Crisis Communication
- If asked to address a negative review or PR issue:
  1. Acknowledge the situation honestly
  2. Keep it short and factual
  3. Take the conversation offline when possible
  4. Never be defensive or dismissive
  5. Flag for owner review before posting

### Trending Topics / Newsjacking
- Only reference trends that naturally connect to the business
- Never force a connection (feels inauthentic)
- Act fast — trends have a 24-48 hour window
- When in doubt, skip it — forced trend posts do more harm than good
